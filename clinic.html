Tracking a referral through review

Several weeks after your referral was sent, you call for an update. “Under review” leaves you with the same question you started with. Has anyone checked the referral? Is the team waiting for more information? Has a decision already been made? In occupational health, that call can reach an administrator or a doctor’s personal assistant (PA). I designed this example around the answer either should be able to give: the last completed step, what is outstanding and when someone will check again.

England’s elective referral queue

NHS England reported a median wait of 14.2 weeks for incomplete consultant-led elective pathways at the end of December 2024. Across those pathways, 58.9% had waited up to 18 weeks. These national figures describe an elective waiting list. They cannot identify which stage delayed an occupational health referral. Why use one shared tracker?

A caller might speak to an administrator on Monday and a doctor’s personal assistant on Friday. If the latest update sits in Monday’s email, Friday’s colleague has to find it before they can answer a simple question: “Are you still waiting for my manager to reply?”

I chose Microsoft Lists, a Microsoft 365 tool that lets colleagues maintain a shared table. In this design, each referral has one row. When something happens, the colleague handling it records the date, what changed, what is still outstanding and when someone will follow up. Both teams can look up the same referral during a call.

For example, when a manager sends the information requested, the team can update the row to “Ready for clinical review”. The next colleague can then tell the caller that the reply has arrived and explain what happens next.

How I would test it

Give two colleagues the same fictional case on the same date. Can both identify the last completed step, explain what is outstanding and give the same follow-up date? During a pilot, track the proportion of open cases with an up-to-date milestone and how often callers need a second call to get an answer.

My design decision

I designed the shared record around the questions I handle in occupational health administration. The example covers five updates to one fictional referral. A pilot would be needed to measure time saved or fewer repeat calls.
