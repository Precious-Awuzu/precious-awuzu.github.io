Investigating customer support handovers

A customer advisor resolves a difficult case, but the customer calls back. Was the issue unusually complex, or did information get lost when the case changed hands? Drawing on my customer-service background, this analysis uses a small fictional extract to show how I would investigate.

Repeat contacts by number of handovers

One handover2/5
Two or more4/5

Each group contains five synthetic cases. The bars show how many customers contacted support again within seven days.

How to reproduce the comparison

Start with one row per resolved case. Group cases by the number of team handovers. Within each group, count cases with a repeat contact within seven days and divide by all resolved cases in that group.

Download the 15 synthetic cases (CSV)

What the pattern can tell a manager

The example makes multiple-handover cases worth inspecting first. In a real review I would compare cases of similar complexity, give each case a full seven days of follow-up and read the handover notes before recommending a process change.
