Data and AI governance · Synthetic case study

Auditing AI-assisted support triage

A service desk is considering an AI score to prioritise tickets for human review. If tickets written in fewer words receive lower scores, an urgent issue could sit in the queue. This synthetic audit makes that trade-off visible.

How a UK model audit measures referrals

DWP reported that Universal Credit advance requests from people aged 16–24 were 1.67 times as likely to be referred for review as requests from people aged 35–44 in 2024–25. For ages 55–65 the ratio was 2.80. These are referral-rate ratios. A missed-case rate requires labels for cases the model passed over. How the audit works

The fixture contains 16 invented tickets, eight per writing style. Four in each group require escalation. Compare each score with the selected threshold, then divide the missed escalations by the four high-priority tickets in that group.

Decision before a pilot

At 0.6, three of four high-priority brief tickets are missed, compared with one of four detailed tickets. Lowering the threshold adds work for reviewers. Before using a real system, sample historic tickets, have reviewers independently label urgency, check disagreements and test the available review capacity. Writing style here is an illustrative grouping. These invented scores cannot establish real-world bias.
