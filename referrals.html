NHS referral demand by week, priority, specialty and commissioner

I analysed 1,674,089 referrals recorded from 1 to 28 September 2025 across 62 specialties and 114 commissioner entries. Routine referrals made up 66.5% of demand overall and 85.2% in orthopaedics. The ten highest-volume commissioners accounted for 29.35% of all referrals.

Measuring incoming demand

Each of the 35,631 rows groups referrals by week, commissioner, priority, specialty and clinic type. I summed the referral-count field by week. Counting the rows would measure the number of reported groups.

The three comparison weeks averaged 398,158 referrals. Using that average as the denominator puts the peak 20.5% higher.

Which services received routine referrals?

The extract recorded 1,112,800 routine referrals, 297,683 two-week-wait referrals and 263,606 urgent referrals. The mix varied across specialties.

Where was referral volume concentrated?

The ten largest commissioner entries accounted for 491,305 referrals. North East London (A3A8R) recorded 74,624, followed by North West London (W2U3Z) with 70,213. Comparing referral rates across areas would require population denominators.

What a service manager could investigate

To understand the peak, I would compare each specialty’s volume that week with its own three-week average. That would show which specialties contributed most of the additional demand.

The extract ends on 28 September and contains no appointment dates or staffing figures. Estimating how long patients waited would require linked pathway records.
